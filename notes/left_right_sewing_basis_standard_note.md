# Left-Right Sewing Basis for Heavy-Pair Local Amplitudes

## Problem Definition

We consider local amplitudes with a distinguished pair of heavy external
particles on legs \(1,2\).  The goal is to construct a basis of the reduced
local amplitude space by sewing a left three-point open-current block to a
right residual block.  The left block contains the heavy-pair spin data.  The
right block contains the remaining local kinematics and spin data of the full
amplitude.  The final basis elements are ordinary full amplitudes obtained
after all open current slots are symmetrically contracted.

Fix the following data:

1. the common heavy-pair spin \(\ell\) on legs \(1,2\);
2. the right-side spins \(R=\{s_3,\ldots,s_n\}\);
3. the set of massive external legs;
4. the right-side polarization sector;
5. the amplitude bracket dimension \(d_{\rm amp}\).

Let
\[
\mathcal P_d
\]
be the vector space of local Lorentz-covariant spinor polynomials in this
sector before quotienting by on-shell relations.  Let
\[
\mathcal I_{\rm os}
\]
be the subspace generated by the EoM relations, Schouten identities, momentum
conservation, and the massive-to-massless relabeling relations used in the
reduction pipeline.  The target reduced amplitude space is the direct sum over
the heavy-pair polarization labels with the right polarization fixed:
\[
\mathcal A_d
{}=
\bigoplus_{\rho_1,\rho_2}
\mathcal A_d(\rho_1,\rho_2;\rho_R).
\]
Here \(\rho_R\) is the specified right-side polarization list.  This is the
space compared to the CF reference in the implementation, where the first two
polarization entries are summed over the allowed heavy-pair range.  Before
splitting by heavy polarizations, the same quotient may be written as
\[
\mathcal A_d
{}=
\mathcal P_d/\mathcal I_{\rm os}.
\]
All span statements below are statements in this heavy-polarization-summed
quotient with fixed right polarization.

The CF block enters as an external verification basis.  The independent
CF-block construction gives a reference matrix in the same reduced quotient.
For each tested sector we compare the sewing matrix \(M_{\rm sew}\), the CF
matrix \(M_{\rm CF}\), and the joined matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
M_{\rm CF}\\
M_{\rm sew}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The verification criterion is equality of the three ranks.  This confirms that
the sewn structures are independent after selecting a rank-maximal subset and
span the same reduced local amplitude space as the established CF basis in the
heavy-polarization-summed sector.

The construction itself is the left-right sewing construction.  The CF block
is the verifier used to test the resulting basis.

The main code-level names used in the examples are:

| name | meaning |
| --- | --- |
| \(d_{\rm amp}\) | displayed bracket dimension of the sewn amplitude |
| `CodeDim` | dimension parameter passed to `ConstructIndepCFBlock` after the package conversion |
| `mass` | list of massive physical legs |
| `polarization` | right-side polarization list in the sewing input; CF comparison sums over heavy-pair polarizations |
| reflected labels | square-spinor representatives, such as \(8,7,6,5\) in four-point examples |
| \(J\) | formal transmitted-current slot label |
| \(a,b\) | temporary massless auxiliary labels used to realize formal \(J\)-slots |
| \(x,y\) | closed heavy-pair scalar generators, implemented through the `Xhard` and `Xsoft` basis |
| CF rank comparison | reduced-coordinate span test against `ConstructIndepCFBlock` |

## Angular-Momentum Decomposition

The heavy-pair little-group representation is
\[
V_\ell\otimes V_\ell .
\]
Finite-dimensional \(SU(2)\) representations over \(\mathbb C\) are
semisimple, so this tensor product decomposes into irreducible transmitted
angular-momentum channels.  The same decomposition applies to the local
polynomial space carrying the heavy-pair action.  If the quotient ideal
\(\mathcal I_{\rm os}\) is stable under this action, then the decomposition
descends to the reduced amplitude space:
\[
\mathcal A_d
{}=
\bigoplus_J \mathcal A_{d,J}.
\]
The component \(\mathcal A_{d,J}\) consists of the structures whose indices
transmitted from the heavy pair to the right block transform in the spin-\(J\)
channel.

The reduction map used for coefficient comparison is compatible with this
decomposition.  The massive-to-massless relabeling \(2n+1-i\mapsto i\) fixes a
spinor-coordinate representative for the same little-group tensor.  The EoM,
Schouten, and momentum-conservation relations are generated by invariant
spinor contractions and momentum identities.  Hence the full reduction map is
an \(SU(2)\)-intertwiner for the heavy-pair action.  It sends each transmitted
\(J\)-component to the corresponding reduced \(J\)-component and does not mix
different transmitted channels.

This decomposition determines the left problem.  For each transmitted
\(J\), the heavy-pair side is the three-object open-current problem
\[
\ell+\ell+J .
\]
The third object is a formal current slot carrying the \(12\)-channel angular
momentum.  Its momentum is
\[
Q=p_1+p_2 .
\]
The local amplitude basis is then assembled by pairing this left current with
a right residual coefficient object carrying the dual formal \(J\)-slots.

Thus the basis construction is organized as
\[
\mathcal A_{12+X}^{(d)}
{}=
\sum_J
\mu_J\!\left(
\mathcal L_{\ell,J}\otimes\mathcal R_{J,d}
\right),
\]
where \(\mathcal L_{\ell,J}\) is the left open-current space,
\(\mathcal R_{J,d}\) is the right residual coefficient space, and
\(\mu_J\) is the symmetric contraction of all matching current slots.

## Left Three-Point Open Basis

Write the open current slot as \(\cdot\).  The two closed heavy-pair scalar
structures are denoted by
\[
x,\qquad y.
\]
They are the two independent scalar generators in the three-point heavy-pair
closed sector.  In the code these correspond to the rotated `Xhard` and
`Xsoft` sectors.  Their normalization is irrelevant for the basis count and
span.

For the spin-one-half pair, a concrete convention is
\[
x=[12]+\langle12\rangle,\qquad
y=[12]-\langle12\rangle .
\]
After the four-point massive-square relabeling used in the examples, the
square partner of leg \(1\) is \(8\) and the square partner of leg \(2\) is
\(7\).  In that convention the same two closed structures can appear in logs
as linear combinations of \(\langle12\rangle\) and \([78]\), up to the chosen
normalization of `Xhard` and `Xsoft`.

For equal heavy-pair spin \(\ell\), set
\[
N=2\ell,\qquad m=\min(J,N),\qquad r=N-m .
\]
The left open-current structures are
\[
\mathcal A^{(\ell)}_{J;a,b,k}
{}=
\langle1\cdot\rangle^{a}[1\cdot]^{m-a}
\langle2\cdot\rangle^{b}[2\cdot]^{m-b}
x^{r-k}y^k
\bigl(\langle Q\cdot\rangle[Q\cdot]\bigr)^{\max(J-N,0)},
\]
with
\[
a,b=0,\ldots,m,\qquad k=0,\ldots,r .
\]
The factors have the following roles.

1. The powers of \(\langle1\cdot\rangle,[1\cdot]\) attach \(m\) spin slots of
leg \(1\) to the transmitted current.
2. The powers of \(\langle2\cdot\rangle,[2\cdot]\) attach \(m\) spin slots of
leg \(2\) to the transmitted current.
3. The factor \(x^{r-k}y^k\) closes the remaining \(r\) heavy-pair slots.
4. When \(J>N\), the factor
\[
\bigl(\langle Q\cdot\rangle[Q\cdot]\bigr)^{J-N}
\]
raises the transmitted current spin beyond the number of heavy spin slots.

The formula follows from the invariant ring of the equal-spin three-object
problem.  Each heavy leg supplies \(N=2\ell\) symmetric spinor slots.  The
current can absorb \(m=\min(J,N)\) slots from each heavy leg.  The remaining
heavy-pair slots are scalar slots.  In the three-point quotient with
\(Q=p_1+p_2\), the scalar heavy-pair sector is generated by the two
inequivalent contractions \(x,y\).  Their degree-\(r\) symmetric products give
\(x^{r-k}y^k\).  Once all heavy slots have been attached, additional
transmitted angular momentum is carried by the unique spin-one current-raising
bilinear \(\langle Q\cdot\rangle[Q\cdot]\).  This gives the displayed basis
and the count \((m+1)^2(r+1)\).

For a spin-one-half heavy pair, \(N=1\).  The \(J=0\) structures are the closed
pair
\[
x,\qquad y.
\]
For every \(J\ge1\), the four open structures are
\[
[1\cdot]\langle2\cdot\rangle
\bigl(\langle Q\cdot\rangle[Q\cdot]\bigr)^{J-1},
\]
\[
\langle1\cdot\rangle[2\cdot]
\bigl(\langle Q\cdot\rangle[Q\cdot]\bigr)^{J-1},
\]
\[
[1\cdot][2\cdot]
\bigl(\langle Q\cdot\rangle[Q\cdot]\bigr)^{J-1},
\]
\[
\langle1\cdot\rangle\langle2\cdot\rangle
\bigl(\langle Q\cdot\rangle[Q\cdot]\bigr)^{J-1}.
\]

For a spin-three-halves heavy pair, \(N=3\).  For \(J=0,1,2\), the powers of
\(x,y\) have total degrees \(3,2,1\), respectively.  For \(J\ge3\), all heavy
spin slots are attached to the current and the stable open basis is multiplied
by
\[
\bigl(\langle Q\cdot\rangle[Q\cdot]\bigr)^{J-3}.
\]
At \(J=3\) there are sixteen open structures,
\[
\langle1\cdot\rangle^a[1\cdot]^{3-a}
\langle2\cdot\rangle^b[2\cdot]^{3-b},
\qquad a,b=0,1,2,3.
\]

The current proof covers the equal-spin family \(\ell+\ell+J\).  The unequal
spin problem \(\ell_1+\ell_2+J\) should be treated as a separate extension.
The natural extension is to use independent slot counts \(N_1=2\ell_1\) and
\(N_2=2\ell_2\), attach \(\min(J,N_i)\) slots on each side, and rederive the
closed scalar sector and current-raising factors for the unmatched slots.

## Right Residual Formal \(J\)-Tableaux

For fixed \(J\), the right residual block is represented by direct formal
\(J\)-slot semistandard Young tableaux.  The alphabet is ordered as
\[
1<2<J<3<4<\cdots .
\]
Rows are weakly increasing and columns are strictly increasing.  The tableau is
split into an angular part and a square part.  The angular part is converted
to spinor brackets after the prescribed Hodge dual.  The square part is
converted directly.

The supplemental labels \(1,2\) in this formal right tableau are not physical
right particles.  They record the heavy-pair supplemental angular column that
can appear on the right after the left block has left open current slots to be
contracted.  The formal label \(J\) records an open current slot that must be
sewn to a left current slot.

The direct formal rules are:

1. angular columns containing \(1;J\) or \(2;J\) are rejected;
2. a retained \(J\)-column must pair \(J\) with a physical right label;
3. supplemental labels \(1,2\) are allowed only on the angular side;
4. square-side supplemental labels are rejected;
5. the number and type of formal \(J\)-slots must match the selected left
record;
6. after conversion to brackets, the amplitude metadata must match the target
spin, mass, polarization, and dimension sector.

The Hodge rule is applied only to the angular part.  In the four-point example
with right physical labels \(3,4\), the useful rules include
\[
\{1,2\}\mapsto\{3,4\},\qquad
\{J,3\}\mapsto\{J,4\},\qquad
\{J,4\}\mapsto\{J,3\}.
\]
Labels with primes in older notes are written as numeric reflected labels in
the code-level output; for example \(6\) is the square partner of \(3\) in a
four-point massless relabeling convention.

In the four-point convention the reflected square labels are
\[
1\leftrightarrow8,\qquad 2\leftrightarrow7,\qquad
3\leftrightarrow6,\qquad 4\leftrightarrow5 .
\]
The final massless reduction sends
\[
8\mapsto1,\qquad 7\mapsto2,\qquad
6\mapsto3,\qquad 5\mapsto4 .
\]

The four-point matrix example below uses the bracket-level sewn record
\[
A_{\rm R}=\langle23\rangle\langle4J\rangle[24][6J].
\]
This record is printed after the package's direct and auxiliary right-backend
processing and after straightening to bracket form.  It is the expression used
for the explicit contraction and rank matrix.

For practicing the direct formal tableau rules themselves, a simple
semistandard representative is
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
J&J&4\\
4&4&6
\end{array}.
\]
The vertical bar separates the angular part from the square part.  The angular
column Hodge-dualizes as
\[
\begin{array}{c}
J\\
4
\end{array}
\mapsto
\langle J3\rangle.
\]
The square columns give \([J4]\) and \([46]\).  Thus this tableau produces
\[
\langle J3\rangle[J4][46].
\]
It has two formal \(J\)-entries, both paired with physical labels, and no
square-side supplemental \(1,2\) labels.  It is therefore in the scope of the
direct formal tableau lift theorem.

The right residual block keeps the local kinematics of the original full
amplitude.  It is not a separate physical on-shell amplitude containing an
external current.  Its purpose is to carry the coefficient data dual to the
left open-current representation.

## Auxiliary-Particle Realization

The direct formal \(J\)-slot tableaux can be realized through two temporary
massless auxiliary particles \(a,b\).  They are inserted before the physical
right labels in the auxiliary alphabet:
\[
a<b<3<4<\cdots .
\]
The convention is
\[
S_a=S_b.
\]
The common auxiliary spin is not chosen freely.  It is determined by the
formal tableau that is being lifted.

Let \(T\) be an admissible direct formal two-row residual tableau.  Let
\(N_J(T)\) be the total number of formal \(J\)-entries in \(T\).  Let
\(N_{12}^{\rm ang}(T)\) be the number of supplemental angular columns
\(1;2\).  The auxiliary lift uses
\[
n_a(T)=n_b(T)
{}=
\frac{N_J(T)}{2}+N_{12}^{\rm ang}(T)
\]
auxiliary slots.  The massless auxiliary spin is the spin corresponding to
this common slot count in the ordinary on-shell filling convention.

The lift is constructed as follows.

1. Every sewing target produced by a left \(\ell+\ell+J\) open structure has
an even total number of right \(J\)-slots:
\[
N_J(T)=2m+2\max(J-N,0).
\]
2. In an admissible two-row direct formal tableau, every surviving formal
\(J\)-entry lies in the top row and is paired with a physical lower entry.
3. The top-row \(J\)-entries form a weakly ordered block.  Replace the first
half by \(a\) and the second half by \(b\).
4. Replace each supplemental angular column \(1;2\) by the auxiliary angular
column \(a;b\).
5. Leave all physical entries unchanged.

The resulting auxiliary tableau is semistandard.  Column strictness is
preserved because \(a,b\) are smaller than all physical right labels.  Row
weak ordering is preserved because the replacement is made in the order
\(a\le b\).  The numbers of \(a\) and \(b\) are equal by construction.

The translation back to the formal right tableau sends a single auxiliary
occurrence paired with a physical label to \(J\), and sends an angular
\(a;b\) column to the supplemental \(1;2\) column.  This translation recovers
the original formal tableau \(T\).

This proves that the equal-spin auxiliary convention realizes every direct
formal right tableau needed by the sewing construction in every sector with
even \(N_J(T)\).

For the direct tableau practice example above, there are two formal
\(J\)-slots and no supplemental angular \(1;2\) column:
\[
N_J(T)=2,\qquad N_{12}^{\rm ang}(T)=0.
\]
Thus
\[
n_a(T)=n_b(T)=1.
\]
The lift replaces one top-row \(J\) by \(a\) and the other by \(b\).  Translating
the two single auxiliary occurrences back to formal \(J\)-slots recovers the
same direct formal tableau.

## Sewing Prescription

For each \(J\), define
\[
\mu_J:
\mathcal L_{\ell,J}\otimes\mathcal R_{J,d}
\longrightarrow
\mathcal A_d
\]
by summing over all pairings of matching formal current slots:
\[
\mu_J(L\otimes R)
{}=
\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n_J}}
L^{I_1\cdots I_{n_J}}
R_{I_{\sigma(1)}\cdots I_{\sigma(n_J)}} .
\]
The same rule is applied in the angle and square sectors of the spinor
expression.  The sum is a full symmetric contraction.  Repeated pairings are
kept with their multiplicity.  No averaging is applied.

The default \(Q\)-choice is
\[
Q\to p_2 .
\]
Other choices are allowed as conventions.  If one uses
\[
Q\to p_1+p_2,
\]
the result is a linear expansion of the same left basis element.  The two
terms are not treated as separate basis directions.

In the four-point examples this convention must be combined with the massive
square relabeling.  A square spinor from leg \(1\) is written with reflected
label \(8\), while a square spinor from leg \(2\) is written with reflected
label \(7\).  Thus a left factor may contain \([8J]\) or \([7J]\) even though
the physical heavy legs are still \(1,2\).  The final reduction maps
\(8\mapsto1\) and \(7\mapsto2\).

The output of the construction is a list of sewn records containing the left
structure, the right formal tableau or its auxiliary realization, the raw
sewn amplitude, and the reduced amplitude.  A rank-maximal subset of these
records is selected as the sewing basis.

## Reduction and Verification

The verification pipeline maps both sewn amplitudes and CF amplitudes to the
same reduced massless-limit coordinate space.

1. Convert massive reflected labels to their massless labels using the
standard relabeling \(2n+1-i\mapsto i\).
2. Apply the same `ReduceSt` reduction used in the CF-block pipeline.
3. Collect the common reduced monomial basis using `Poly2Singlet`.
4. Build one coefficient row for each reduced amplitude.
5. Compare the ranks of \(M_{\rm sew}\), \(M_{\rm CF}\), and the joined
matrix.

The pass criterion is
\[
\operatorname{rank}M_{\rm sew}
{}=
\operatorname{rank}M_{\rm CF}
{}=
\operatorname{rank}
\begin{pmatrix}
M_{\rm CF}\\
M_{\rm sew}
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The first equality says that sewing produces the same number of independent
directions as the CF reference.  The second equality says that adding the sewn
rows to the CF rows does not enlarge the span.  Together they show that the
sewn records contain an independent complete basis after selecting independent
rows.

The code dimension used by `ConstructIndepCFBlock` is not always the displayed
bracket dimension \(d_{\rm amp}\).  In the four-point example below,
\[
d_{\rm amp}=4
\]
corresponds to
\[
\mathrm{CodeDim}=8
\]
in the CF call.  The sewing comparison routines perform this conversion before
calling `ConstructIndepCFBlock`.

## Mathematical Proof

**Theorem 0, CF independent completeness.**  For the fixed target sector, the
CF construction returns a set
\[
\mathcal C_d=\{C_\alpha\}
\]
whose images form a basis of the reduced amplitude space:
\[
\operatorname{Span}\{\overline C_\alpha\}
{}=
\mathcal A_d .
\]
This theorem is used as an input theorem and as the source of the verification
reference basis.

**Lemma 1, \(J\)-channel decomposition.**  The heavy-polarization-summed
reduced target space with fixed right polarization decomposes as
\[
\mathcal A_d
{}=
\bigoplus_J\mathcal A_{d,J}.
\]

**Proof.**  The pre-quotient polynomial space \(\mathcal P_d\) is a finite
dimensional \(SU(2)\) representation in the fixed dimension sector.
Finite-dimensional \(SU(2)\) representations over \(\mathbb C\) are
semisimple, so \(\mathcal P_d\) decomposes into \(J\)-isotypic components.  The
relations in \(\mathcal I_{\rm os}\) are generated by EoM, Schouten identities,
momentum conservation, and the massive-to-massless representative map.  These
relations are written using invariant tensors or momentum identities and are
therefore equivariant under the heavy-pair \(SU(2)\) action.  Hence
\(\mathcal I_{\rm os}\) is an \(SU(2)\)-subrepresentation.  The quotient and
the subsequent reduced coordinate map inherit the same decomposition.

**Lemma 2, left open-current basis.**  For equal heavy-pair spin \(\ell\), the
structures
\[
\mathcal A^{(\ell)}_{J;a,b,k}
\]
given above form a basis of the left open-current space
\(\mathcal L_{\ell,J}\).

**Proof.**  Each heavy leg has \(N=2\ell\) symmetric spin slots.  At fixed
\(J\), exactly \(m=\min(J,N)\) slots on each leg can attach directly to the
current.  The \(m\) attached slots on leg \(1\) split into \(a\) angle slots
and \(m-a\) square slots, giving \(m+1\) choices.  The same applies to leg
\(2\).  The remaining \(r=N-m\) heavy-pair slots are closed and are generated
by the degree-\(r\) monomials \(x^{r-k}y^k\).  The independence of \(x,y\) and
their symmetric products follows from the three-point heavy-pair scalar
invariant ring: in the equal-spin sector it is generated by the two
inequivalent heavy-pair scalar contractions represented by \(x,y\).  If
\(J>N\), the extra current slots are supplied by the unique raising factor
\(\langle Q\cdot\rangle[Q\cdot]\), the only spin-one bilinear with one angle
and one square open current slot compatible with the three-point kinematics.
The product basis therefore spans \(\mathcal L_{\ell,J}\).  The count is
\((m+1)^2(r+1)\), matching the implemented left basis.

**Lemma 3, branching-factorization.**  For each fixed target sector and each
transmitted \(J\), the CF/SSYT basis of \(\mathcal A_{d,J}\) admits a
triangular decomposition into left current tableaux and direct formal
right-residual tableaux:
\[
\mathcal A_{d,J}
\simeq
\operatorname{Span}\{
\mu_J(L_i\otimes R_\alpha^i)
\}.
\]
Here \(L_i\) runs over the left basis of Lemma 2, and \(R_\alpha^i\) runs
exactly over the direct formal residual tableaux satisfying the Hodge,
slot-count, metadata, and supplemental-label filters.

**Proof.**  Start from an ordinary CF/SSYT tableau in the \(J\)-isotypic
component.  Branching with respect to the heavy-pair \(SU(2)\) action separates
the boxes carrying heavy-pair spin from the boxes carrying the remaining
right-side data.  The heavy-pair boxes straighten to exactly one element of
the left basis in Lemma 2: open boxes give the angle and square current slots,
closed heavy-pair boxes give powers of \(x,y\), and excess transmitted boxes
give powers of \(\langle Q\cdot\rangle[Q\cdot]\).  Removing this left factor
leaves the coefficient tableau on the right.  The removed open boxes are
recorded as formal \(J\)-entries, and removed closed heavy-pair angular columns
are recorded as supplemental \(1;2\) columns.  The ordinary SSYT Hodge rule on
the angular part becomes the formal \(J\)-Hodge rule stated above.  Therefore
every branched CF tableau produces one allowed pair \((L_i,R_\alpha^i)\).

Conversely, given an allowed pair \((L_i,R_\alpha^i)\), replace every formal
\(J\)-entry in the right tableau by the corresponding open box of the left
current tableau and replace every supplemental \(1;2\) angular column by the
closed heavy-pair scalar column represented in \(L_i\).  The target-count and
metadata filters are precisely the conditions that the reconstructed filling
has the original spin, mass, polarization, and dimension data.  Semistandard
straightening puts the reconstructed filling into the ordinary CF/SSYT basis.
Thus every allowed formal residual tableau comes from a branched CF tableau.

The triangular order is the following standard-monomial key.  First fix the
transmitted \(J\)-content.  Then compare the left current content
\[
(a,b,k,J-N)_+
\]
lexicographically, where \(a,b,k\) are the labels in the left basis formula.
Finally compare the right residual semistandard filling lexicographically by
columns from left to right.  In this order, ordinary SSYT straightening is
triangular: the product of a left standard monomial and a right standard
residual monomial has as its leading key the tableau obtained by inserting the
formal \(J\)-slots back into the heavy-pair boxes, and every straightening term
has a strictly smaller right residual filling at fixed left key or a smaller
left key.

The quotient generators in \(\mathcal I_{\rm os}\) respect the same leading
key.  Schouten relations are the Plucker straightening relations and are
triangular in the standard monomial order.  EoM and momentum-conservation
relations replace nonstandard contractions by linear combinations with lower
standard keys after the massive-to-massless representative is chosen.  Since
the reduction map is \(SU(2)\)-equivariant by Lemma 1, these replacements do
not mix transmitted \(J\)-content.  Therefore the leading key of a branched
standard tableau cannot be cancelled by higher terms from a different
branched pair.

The map from branched CF tableaux to pairs \((L_i,R_\alpha^i)\) is therefore
triangular with nonzero diagonal.  It is injective after straightening and
after quotienting by \(\mathcal I_{\rm os}\).  The symmetric sewing map
\(\mu_J\) is exactly the evaluation pairing that contracts the formal current
boxes introduced by the branching.  This proves the triangular decomposition.

**Lemma 4, formal residual completeness.**  The direct formal \(J\)-slot SSYT
rules give a basis of the residual coefficient space \(\mathcal R_{J,d}\) in
each \(J\)-channel.

**Proof.**  In the branching-factorization lemma, the right residual tableaux
are exactly the coefficient tableaux dual to the left current basis.  The
triangularity argument gives independence, and the reconstruction direction
gives spanning.  Therefore they form a basis of \(\mathcal R_{J,d}\).

**Lemma 5, evenness of right \(J\)-slots.**  Every right target produced by a
left \(\ell+\ell+J\) open structure has an even total number of formal
\(J\)-entries.

**Proof.**  With \(N=2\ell\), \(m=\min(J,N)\), and
\(q=\max(J-N,0)\), the two heavy legs supply \(2m\) direct current slots.  The
factor \((\langle Q\cdot\rangle[Q\cdot])^q\) supplies \(2q\) more current
slots.  Hence
\[
N_J=2m+2q,
\]
which is even.

**Lemma 6, position of formal \(J\)-entries.**  In an admissible two-row direct
formal residual tableau, every surviving \(J\)-entry lies in the top row and
is paired with a physical lower entry.

**Proof.**  A bottom-row \(J\) would require a smaller top entry.  The possible
supplemental top entries are \(1,2\).  Angular columns \(1;J\) and \(2;J\) are
rejected by the formal Hodge admissibility rule.  Square-side supplemental
labels are rejected by the target filter.  Therefore a retained \(J\)-entry
cannot occur in the bottom row or with a supplemental partner.  It must occur
in the top row above a physical label.

**Theorem R-direct, equal-spin auxiliary lift.**  Every admissible two-row
direct formal residual tableau in the sewing sector has an equal-spin
two-auxiliary lift.

**Proof.**  By Lemma 5, the number of formal \(J\)-entries is even.  By Lemma
6, these entries occur in the top row above physical lower entries.  Since the
formal top row is weakly ordered in the alphabet
\[
1<2<J<3<4<\cdots ,
\]
all top-row supplemental entries occur before the \(J\)-block and all top-row
physical entries occur after it.  The target filters allow supplemental
entries only through complete angular \(1;2\) columns.  Lift each such column
to \(a;b\).  Then replace the first half of the \(J\)-block by \(a\) and the
second half by \(b\), with
\[
a<b<3<4<\cdots .
\]
The lifted top row has the form
\[
a\cdots a\, b\cdots b\, 3\cdots ,
\]
after omitting absent segments, and is therefore weakly ordered.  Column
strictness holds because \(a,b\) are smaller than all physical lower entries
and the lifted supplemental column is \(a;b\).  The number of \(a\) entries
equals the number of \(b\) entries and is
\[
\frac{N_J(T)}{2}+N_{12}^{\rm ang}(T).
\]
The auxiliary labels therefore have equal spin.  Translating single auxiliary
occurrences back to \(J\) and \(a;b\) back to \(1;2\) recovers the original
formal tableau.

**Lemma 7, symmetric sewing is the invariant pairing.**  The map \(\mu_J\) is
the \(SU(2)\)-invariant pairing between the spin-\(J\) current representation
carried by the left block and the dual formal \(J\)-slot representation carried
by the right block.

**Proof.**  Both sides are symmetric tensor realizations of the same
spin-\(J\) representation and its dual.  The full sum over all permutations of
matching slots is the invariant contraction between these symmetric tensor
representations, up to an overall normalization.  Bracket-level sewing applies
this contraction separately to angle-current slots and square-current slots.
Keeping duplicate permutations is the unnormalized symmetric-tensor pairing.
Changing this normalization rescales rows but does not change the span in
\(\mathcal A_d\).

**Lemma 8, coefficient-space surjectivity.**  For each \(J\), the sewing map
\[
\mu_J:
\mathcal L_{\ell,J}\otimes\mathcal R_{J,d}
\longrightarrow
\mathcal A_{d,J}
\]
is surjective.

**Proof.**  This is the image statement of the branching-factorization lemma.
Choose a basis \(L_i\) of \(\mathcal L_{\ell,J}\) and the corresponding
residual coefficient basis \(R_\alpha^i\) of \(\mathcal R_{J,d}\).  Every
element of \(\mathcal A_{d,J}\) has an expansion
\[
A_J
{}=
\sum_{i,\alpha} c_{i\alpha}\,
\mu_J(L_i\otimes R_\alpha^i).
\]
Lemma 7 identifies \(\mu_J\) with the invariant contraction implementing this
dual pairing.  Therefore the image of \(\mu_J\) is the whole
\(\mathcal A_{d,J}\).

**Theorem 1, relative completeness of the sewing basis.**  Assume Theorem 0,
Lemma 1, Lemma 2, Lemma 3, Lemma 4, Lemma 5, Lemma 6, Theorem R-direct, Lemma
7, and Lemma 8.  Then the sewn amplitudes span the target reduced local amplitude
space:
\[
\operatorname{Span}\{\overline A_{\rm sew}\}
{}=
\mathcal A_d .
\]

**Proof.**  Lemma 1 decomposes the target reduced space into \(J\)-channels.
Lemma 2 gives the complete left current space \(\mathcal L_{\ell,J}\) in each
channel.  Lemma 4 gives the complete right residual coefficient space
\(\mathcal R_{J,d}\).  Theorem R-direct shows that every required direct
formal residual tableau has an equal-spin auxiliary realization.  Lemma 7
identifies sewing as the invariant pairing, and Lemma 8 gives surjectivity
onto each \(J\)-channel.  Therefore
\[
\sum_J
\mu_J\!\left(\mathcal L_{\ell,J}\otimes\mathcal R_{J,d}\right)
{}=
\bigoplus_J\mathcal A_{d,J}
{}=
\mathcal A_d .
\]
Thus the sewn amplitudes span the target space.  The CF basis is independently
complete by Theorem 0, so equality of reduced ranks with the CF matrix verifies
that the selected sewn rows form an independent basis of the same space.

## Operational Specification

To reproduce the construction in a new sector:

1. Fix \(\ell\), \(R\), the massive legs, the right polarization sector, and
\(d_{\rm amp}\).
2. Enumerate the left open-current structures for all \(J\) needed at this
dimension.
3. For each left record, compute the required right formal \(J\)-slot counts.
4. Enumerate direct formal \(J\)-slot right tableaux satisfying the SSYT,
Hodge, target-count, and metadata filters.
5. If an auxiliary realization is needed, lift each direct tableau by the
equal-spin rule \(n_a=n_b=N_J/2+N_{12}^{\rm ang}\).
6. Convert each retained right tableau to \(A_{\rm R}\).
7. Symmetrically contract all matching \(J\)-slots between \(A_{\rm L}\) and
\(A_{\rm R}\).
8. Apply the massive-to-massless relabeling and `ReduceSt`.
9. Build the reduced coefficient matrix and choose rank-independent sewn rows.
10. Compare to `ConstructIndepCFBlock` through the rank criterion above.

The CF comparison is a verification step.  The basis under study is the sewn
basis selected from the left-right construction.
